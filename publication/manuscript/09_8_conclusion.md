## 8. Conclusion

Healthcare RCM organizations do not lack data; they often lack a controlled path from fragmented exports to defensible decisions. A governance-first clean-room architecture addresses that gap by preserving source evidence, detecting duplicate and drift conditions, governing semantic mappings, retaining lineage, reconciling financial relationships, and blocking publication when unresolved issues would make the output misleading.

The synthetic evaluation demonstrates the intended behavior. Dirty inputs were not lost or silently repaired: they were dispositioned, contained, and surfaced, while one unresolved mapping correctly blocked semantic publication. After remediation, the same control logic produced a GREEN result with complete lineage and reconciliation. The core lesson is simple: analytics become decision-ready only after the organization can explain where the data came from, what changed, what remains unresolved, and why the publication gate passed.
