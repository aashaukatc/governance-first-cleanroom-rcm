## Abstract

Healthcare revenue-cycle management (RCM) teams routinely depend on operational exports from electronic medical record, practice-management, clearinghouse, payer, and business-intelligence systems. These exports are often fragmented across report families, inconsistent naming conventions, shifting schemas, duplicated files, uncontrolled spreadsheets, and locally interpreted payer, provider, procedure, denial, and claim-status labels. A dashboard can therefore be technically functional while still being unsuitable for operational decisions because its inputs, transformations, classifications, and exceptions are not governed. This paper presents a governance-first clean-room architecture that treats trust as a prerequisite for analytics rather than as a reporting-layer enhancement. The architecture combines immutable source preservation, SHA-256 file fingerprints, explicit report-family contracts, duplicate detection, schema-drift quarantine, governed cross-reference mappings, row-level lineage, financial reconciliation, and deterministic GREEN/YELLOW/RED publication gates. Unresolved values are not silently converted into permanent “unknown” categories; they remain visible failures until an accountable mapping decision is made. The artifact was developed using a design-science methodology and evaluated through a reproducible synthetic demonstration containing claim-financial, payment, and denial exports. In the dirty scenario, six input files produced one duplicate, one schema-drift exception, one unknown report family, and one unresolved payer mapping; the semantic publication gate correctly returned RED while preserving all source files and producing zero financial reconciliation exceptions. In the remediated scenario, three conforming files produced 180 clean claim records and 20 clean denial records, all with source hashes and no unresolved mappings or reconciliation exceptions; the gate returned GREEN. The contribution is a practical reference architecture and open implementation for converting unreliable administrative exports into explainable, traceable, and decision-ready RCM data without exposing production or patient data.

**Keywords:** healthcare revenue-cycle management; data governance; data quality; clean-room analytics; data lineage; schema drift; semantic mapping; decision support; SQL Server; reproducible data pipelines
